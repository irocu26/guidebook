    Title: Software Architecture - ROS 2 vs PyMAVLink, DDS, and GCS Chaos

    The Concept: You have two primary routes to get your companion computer talking to your Flight Controller (FC): the lightweight PyMAVLink path (sending raw MAVLink packets) or the modern full-blown robotics stack with ROS 2 (using the native DDS Micro-ROS bridge or legacy MAVROS). Picking the right setup comes down to your FC's available RAM and how much overhead your autonomy stack can tolerate.

    Keep things in Mind: - The DDS vs MAVROS trap: To run DDS, your FC needs more than 1MB of RAM/Flash (like STM32H7 chips). If your FC has under 1MB of RAM, the DDS binary isnt recommended by @Ryanf . - Getting overwhelmed by Ground Stations: On Linux, everything technically runs—Mission Planner (running through the Mono runtime), QGroundControl (QGC), MAVProxy, and for quick browser-based setup, mico config (an online configurator) felt pretty convenient too.Make a simple rule early: use MAVProxy in SITL for fast scripting and terminal checks, but once you're tuning real hardware on the field, stick to Mission Planner for rock-solid telemetry and parameter handling. - Flying completely blind on coordinates: When we sent position coordinate commands from our companion computer, we had zero clue what numbers were actually reaching the FC. I realized way too late that you can jump right into ArduPilot's source code, drop custom debug print statements, compile a custom binary, and flash it to watch the raw coordinates land live inside your GCS! For fuck sake do this early on;

    The Jargon: - MAVLink: A lightweight binary telemetry protocol that ArduPilot speaks natively. - PyMAVLink: A Python wrapper library used to create and parse MAVLink messages directly (the lightweight alternative to MAVSDK and the deprecated DroneKit). - MAVROS: A heavy ROS 1/2 node that acts as a translator, turning ROS topics into MAVLink packets over a serial or UDP port. - DDS (Micro-ROS Bridge): Data Distribution Service middleware where ArduPilot natively publishes and subscribes to ROS 2 topics (/ap/...) with no translation node in between (strictly requires >1MB RAM FC like Pixhawk 6C/6X). - GCS (Ground Control Station): Software (Mission Planner, QGC, MAVProxy) used to monitor drone telemetry, adjust parameters, and trigger flight modes.

    The Task (Do It Yourself): - Step 1: Fly a basic autonomous navigation pattern in GUIDED mode: arm, take off, and fly a clean 5x5 meter square (first manually by typing coordinate commands into the MAVProxy terminal, then by writing an automated PyMAVLink script). - Step 2: Clone the ArduPilot repository, navigate to libraries/AP_DDS/, and inject custom debug console messages (gcs().send_text()) into the incoming external navigation topic handlers. - Step 3: Compile your custom binary (./waf configure --board sitl and ./waf copter), launch SITL, and verify that the companion computer's raw coordinates are printing out live on your GCS console. - Question for you: If you're stuck with a budget flight controller running an STM32F4 processor with low RAM, can you use the native DDS Micro-ROS architecture, or are you forced to fall back on MAVROS or PyMAVLink?

    Where to find the answers: - ArduPilot DDS Official Guide: ROS 2 with ArduPilot DDS - PyMAVLink Documentation: PyMAVLink GitHub & Docs - Code Location: libraries/AP_DDS/ in the ArduPilot root directory. - Discord Mentors: Hit up @RyanF on the ArduPilot Discord (#ros).
